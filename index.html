About

I am a Heilbronn Research Fellow at the University of Bristol, funded by the Heilbronn Institute for Mathematical Research. I was previously a Zorn Postdoctoral Fellow at Indiana University Bloomington mentored by Chris Judge, and prior to that, a graduate student at the University of Washington advised by Jayadev Athreya and Farbod Shokrieh. I did my undergraduate in Mechanical Engineering at Columbia University in New York City. After graduating, I worked in New York City for seven years at BuroHappold Engineering. While I was working I started taking math classes at my alma mater, and found some phenomenal guidance. Thank you Professor Gallagher, Professor Khovanov, Professor Weinstein, and Professor Diogo. If you love math and want to talk, or are curious about the nexus of engineering, sustainability, and climate change, feel free to reach out.

Research Interests

I work at the intersection of dynamics, harmonic analysis, and representation theory. I study flat structures and their moduli spaces, hyperbolic manifolds, Lie groups, and more generally metric spaces endowed with some amount of hyperbolicity. The majority of my work involves connecting harmonic analysis to dynamical properties of group actions on these various geometries.

On the one hand, I am interested in arguments that quantify these actions. On the other, I am interested in geometric properties that are connected to dynamical systems, such as a periodic trajectory of a flow. I especially love when these worlds collide - the interplay between these worlds is rich, the questions that emerge are deep and subtle, and the applications are broad: from Hamiltonian mechanics, to statistical mechanics, to quantum mechanics.
